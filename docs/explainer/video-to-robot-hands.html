Video to Robot Hands

A person moves a cup in front of a head-mounted camera. Our job is to make two simulated robot hands move the same cup in the same way. The leaderboard measures how close the simulated cup's path is to the real path, which was recorded with motion capture.

The goal

Copy a real motion into a simulator

The pipeline

Six steps from video to score

Inputs on the left, the score on the right. Each arrow is labeled with the data that flows along it. Box borders show what already works.

What turns into what

The data at each step

INPUT

Video frames

Three head cameras. The colour one finds hands; the two black-and-white ones act like two eyes and give depth.

137–373 frames per episode · colour 2028×1520 · b, c 1280×800
STEP 1

Depth map

For every pixel, how far away it is. FoundationStereo computes it from the two black-and-white cameras.

1600×1000 per frame, metres · depth = 600 px × 0.0755 m ÷ shift between the two images
STEP 1

Pose

Where an object is (x, y, z) and how it is turned. The camera gets one too, because the head moves.

one 4×4 matrix per object per frame, plus one for the camera
STEP 1 OUTPUT

Object path

Every object's pose in every frame, in one fixed world. This is what perception delivers.

frames × objects × 7 numbers (x, y, z + 4-number rotation)
STEP 2 OUTPUT

Hand joints

MediaPipe finds 21 points per hand on the colour camera; our depth lifts them into 3-D.

frames × 2 hands × 21 points × (x, y, z)
STEP 3 OUTPUT

Robot target

What the robot hands should do in each frame. Human hand points are turned into robot wrist poses and finger angles with NVIDIA's own retargeting code.

one Parquet file per episode, exactly 20 frames per second
STEP 4 OUTPUT

Policy

A neural network that drives the robot hands. It learns by trial and error (reinforcement learning) in 4,096 copies of the scene at once.

≈5 MB per episode · about 2 GPU-hours to train
STEP 5 OUTPUT

Simulated path

The official evaluator runs the policy once and records where the simulated objects went. This file is what gets submitted and scored.

same shape as the object path · scored against motion capture
How the score works

Distance per frame, then a curve

The scorer first lines up the first object at the first frame with one rigid move, and never fixes scale. Then, for every frame:

The leaderboard ranks by AUC. It also reports SP-SR (average error within 10 cm and 0.5 rad), MP-SR (every object within 3 cm and 30°), RPE (the error between the objects' relative positions) and MPPE (the error of six points placed 5 cm around each object).

What we tried so far

Tracking works; the robot stage is next

  • worked
    Simulator on a rented GPU. NVIDIA's robot simulator runs on Colab at 45,300 steps/s, so one full policy takes about 1.5–2 hours. The official evaluator also works.
  • failed
    Finding objects from text prompts ("a cup", "a pot"). It misses the pot on black-and-white images and swapped two cups. We replaced it with matching against the object scans.
  • worked
    Scan matching + FoundationPose + camera tracking. 0.809 mean on the 20 public episodes; the twin-cup episode went from 0.10 to 0.87.
  • failed
    Choosing objects by FoundationPose's own confidence. That score can't compare different image regions: a piece of the rack outscored the real cup, 143 to 73.
  • failed
    Hands on the black-and-white cameras. MediaPipe almost never finds them there.
  • worked
    Hands on the colour camera. Both hands found in 173 of 181 and 338 of 373 frames. NVIDIA's hand retargeting works with these 21 points, so we don't need the licensed MANO hand model.
  • built
    Written, not run yet: 3-D hand lifting, the robot-target builder, the one-command pipeline, a shape check that rejects wrong detections, and better camera tracking.
  • built
    A model of the wooden spoon, which has no scan and appears in 10 of 20 eval episodes. Its orientation convention is close to a coin flip, so we will ask the organisers.
  • done
    GPU price research. Cheapest fit: an RTX PRO 6000 on Vast.ai at about $1.10 per hour; the whole campaign comes to about $110.
