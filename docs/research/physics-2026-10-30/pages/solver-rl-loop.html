Solver–RL Loop

The robot policy and a physics solver take turns. The policy trains, we watch where it drops an object, the solver plans that stretch from the robot's own position, and the plan is written back into what the policy trains on. This page shows how the loop works, what it did in today's test, why it barely acted, and a more aggressive version.

1 · How it works now

One episode, three training rounds, two solver turns

The loop runs between training rounds; scoring runs once at the end. Option 3's control arm (O1) trains on R0 the whole time with early stopping and no solver.

Verdict: the rule says "signal", the evidence says the loop did not do it

The rule we fixed in advance (mean ≥ +0.03, no episode ≤ −0.05) is met. But 3 of the 4 winning checkpoints were saved before the loop had changed anything. And option 3's first round was not the same training as the control: the learning rate falls from 3e-4 to 1.5e-4 over each run's own length, so a 30M round falls more than twice as fast as the control's 70M run. The ep2 gap may be that schedule or ep2's usual run-to-run chaos. After the solver turns, option 3 never beat its own first-round best on ep2, ep18 or ep24.

The solver found nothing better

Starting from a robot that is already slipping, its best plan scored the same as the current one, so it stopped after the minimum 6 iterations and changed no frames.

1 window

One failure per turn

Each turn looked at one replay and found one window. A separate analysis shows replays split at 1–2 contact moments; one replay sees only one of them.

4 states

A tiny restart bank

Late and rare

Turns came only at 30M and 50M. By 30M ep2 had already collapsed (0.966 → 0.488), and nothing brought it back.

Loop v2: more turns, harder solves, a fair control

Every change below answers one of the four problems above, or the learning-rate flaw. Each is a hypothesis until a run tests it.

What it would take

  • CPU build and dry run first, then one GPU box. 4 public episodes, one seed, plus 2 extra seeds on ep2 because ep2 is chaotic.
  • Training: 10M shared start + 2 × 60M branches per episode. Solves overlap with training and add perhaps 1–2 h.
  • Estimate: about 7–9 h on one box, roughly $10–15 (estimate, not measured). Nothing is rented without your go-ahead.
  • Pass/fail fixed in advance: the loop branch beats the no-loop branch by ≥ +0.03 on average, no episode ≤ −0.05, and at least half the winning checkpoints come from after a solver turn.

A cheaper first step tests only the learning-rate flaw: the control with the shorter schedule, 3 seeds on ep2, about $3–4. If that alone explains ep2's +0.25, the loop needs v2 to show anything.
