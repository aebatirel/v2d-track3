SUMMARY: DIG 17 is complete. Verdict: hybrid. Mocap markers cannot be the primary tracker for the cups, pot, pitcher, sweeper or dust pan. They should be a correction layer on a model-based tracker. The pot lid is the only moving object a marker-only tracker follows; static racks also hold, but they need no tracker.

Stage C ran on 6 public episodes, 2 of them unseen (13 and 22), using real automatic detections, video-only cameras (stereo VO plus a per-episode cam-a time offset) and detectors trained on other episodes. Inference read no GT; GT was used only to emulate the model-based prior and in the evaluator.

- **Marker-only tracking (coarse init of 20 mm / 5 deg):** the lid has full fixes in 0.98-1.00 of frames, ADD about 9 mm, AUC 0.977. Cups, pot and pitcher are lost at the first grasp or carry (AUC 0.15-0.76), even in ep 22 where all four cup markers face the head.
- **Markers correcting a model-based prior (emulated as GT plus AR(1) noise of 10 mm / 5 deg):** the cup gets 2-or-more-marker fixes in 0.53-0.98 of carried frames. ADD on fix frames drops from 16-24 mm to 6-18 mm. Trajectory AUC gains +0.03 to +0.10 using per-frame fixes, and +0.05 to +0.12 when the corrections are interpolated offline. The unseen episodes match: cup 0.879 -> 0.946 (ep 13) and 0.874 -> 0.975 (ep 22).
- **Cup spin:** single-frame spin choices are unreliable. A temporal vote over fix frames is right in 9/10 runs, and with an abstain rule every decision is right or abstains.

Expected impact [E]: about +0.02 to +0.10 board AUC, central about +0.04. Most of it comes from cup/pot spin and slot-0 anchors in the 10 eval episodes with a cup or pot in slot 0. This assumes a model-based tracker that keeps its error within about 10-20 mm / 5-10 deg; markers cannot re-acquire an object the tracker has lost.

The main limitation is that the prior was emulated, not taken from a real tracker. This run also wrote up the earlier unreported runs, replaced an unsaved carry script with a saved one (it reproduces the earlier numbers exactly), and measured the cost of camera time lag. Nothing is running, and no SlamDunk file other than the notes was edited.

**Answer.** Yes, the CoACD inflation can be removed on the asset side. The evaluator, the CoACD call (decompose_objects true, threshold 0.05) and all physics settings stay as they are; only files named by our reference parquet change. The loader accepts several `<collision>` meshes and primitives per link, decomposes each mesh on its own, and returns a closed convex piece unchanged.

There are two independent causes, of similar size on most pairs:
- **Voxel preprocessing** of every public collision mesh, because all of them fail CoACD's manifold check. It adds about half of L/100 per surface. Watertight or welded input removes it.
- **The concavity tolerance** of 0.025 x L for each mesh handed to CoACD. Only smaller meshes or exact pieces remove it.

The target is the 1.6-4.3 mm floor that even exact colliders show in the GT states. Results against it:
- **Recommended set (REC):** rack and pot split into parts of at most 120 mm, blue cup in 2 parts, lid, beige cup and sweeper whole, all inputs watertight or welded. It measures +0.5 / +1.0 mm median above the floor (A-in-B / B-in-A), against +5.4 / +2.3 mm for the public assets.
- **Finer split (S60r120)** reaches the floor within 0.9 mm in both directions, but at 6-8x the reset contacts.
