### learnings
- The reference calculation is basically correct. Scale is 1.004 (the kit does not correct scale, so a bias would have hurt every episode), frame i is the same moment in perception, reference, rollout and mocap (747/747 rollouts), and quaternion order and slot order are right. The 'everything 15% too small' depth fear is falsified.
- Public mocap covers objects only (Vicon on objects; no hands, no camera). Hands can only be judged with GT-free proxies (jumps, bone lengths, fingers inside objects).
- The kit's scoring has two lotteries that a local number cannot see. (1) Frame-0 alignment: the whole scene is rotated onto the anchor object's frame-0 pose, our frame 0 is the noisiest frame (FoundationPose register(), 1.07° median vs ≈ 0.5°), and in rollouts frame 0 is recorded after the first control step. (2) Registration: our submitted mesh is registered to the hidden, possibly decimated scan with a fixed seed (1_000_003·ep + 101·slot), and near-symmetric meshes can flip 180° (sweeper, board 2).
- Any submitted mesh change needs a multi-seed, multi-host registration-stability check. A single-draw twin A/B is unreliable: even the stable v3 pitcher spans 0.667–0.833 across 30 seeds. Proposed gate: reject if more than 10% of 30 seeds × stand-in hosts land more than 5° from the median pose. It would have rejected v3p and it flags the sweeper (19, 30) and board 2 (25).
- mkref's per-object table snap (±2 cm, mkref.py ≈ l. 963–967) is wrong for objects resting on other objects (pot in rack, lid on pot, cup in rack). Gated snap is a hypothesis to test on CPU, with retraining needed for evals 28, 1 and 14.
- Resting objects that move in our tracks (cup drift, board creep) hurt directly and also fire the grasp lock's onset anchor early. The anchor counts a cup's spin about its own axis; it should measure swing, not spin.
- Our object paths are about half a frame (23–27 ms) late at every stage, independent of tracker iterations. This was already seen on 6 Oct. The pure value is only ≈ +0.003.
- The loss model needs care. true AUC ≈ 0.979 × sim_vs_ref holds only where the reference scores R ≳ 0.93; at high S, T ≈ 1.025·S·R. The implied spoon accuracy is sensitive to that scale (0.80 → 0.73–0.76), so the spoon may cost more than thought. Only ≈ 74% of the pack 3 → 4 gain reached the leaderboard, a hint that the changed eval references (10, 25, 33) are worse than their public twins (one noisy measurement).
- Scoring with the public scan is not an upper bound (ep26 pot: ours 0.947 > scan 0.913). The kit translates the scene on slot 0 and rotates it on the first non-symmetric object, so a slot-0 mesh offset (blue cup ≈ 1 cm along its axis) moves every object.
- Robustness for the competition means: policies whose rollouts agree (0.811 included ≈ +0.027 of draw luck); automatic GT-free triggers in place of per-episode hand fixes (the sponsor runs a held-out evaluation after close; positions are provisional until reproducibility verification); manifest-driven packing; adaptive-n evaluation with pre-registered tie-breaks.
- v3 stays the best measured mesh library (v4 broke tracking, the v5 scan-free rebuild was worse), but it was built with public-scan help. That provenance question was meant for the organisers and was never sent.
- Process: an adversarial verifier per check, writing its own code, caught several over-claims and one silent no-op bug before the owner saw final numbers. Workflow results resume from cache after a session limit.
