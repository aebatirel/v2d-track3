## Explanations worth keeping

- **The scorer:** it aligns the whole scene once, at frame 0, using the first object without a symmetry axis, so that object's frame-0 pose tilts everything after it. It also fits our submitted mesh onto its scan of the real object and reads our poses through that fit. So the submitted mesh changes the pose scores, not only CD-O.
- **Two scores:** the own score measures how well the robot follows our reference (about 0.94 average). The board measures one stored run against real motion. A high own score does not guarantee a high board score.
- **Why copying fails:** physics gives the same result only with the same inputs, and grip force is not visible. Even identical runs of one policy scored 0.26 to 0.92. A feedback policy is what closes that gap.
- **Contact limit:** the solver's capacity for contact equations per step. When a step needs more, the extra are dropped silently and training learns in broken physics. Eval 10 needed more than 512; training at 1024 and saving at 512 raised it from 0.459 to 0.913.
- **Spread between runs:** not randomness. Runs stay within micrometres until a contact event, then split there. Where a policy fails every time, all runs agree: a plan problem.
- **Overlapping collision shapes** caused the largest eval losses. Carving the overlap removed the spread (eval 1 from 0.24 to 0.02; eval 25 from about 0.23 to 0.88).
- **Timing and sampling:** tracking lags by about half a frame (25 to 28 ms), and shifting it fixed 32 of 33 objects. At 20 fps with evenly spaced frames, vibration aliases cannot be separated without random timing or rolling shutter.
- **Why Kalman filters did not help:** a filter removes jitter. Our error is a per-object offset plus slow drift, and a live filter adds lag. A whole-clip smoother gains under 1 %, below the scorer's 1 to 9 cm steps.
- **Why the physics solver stalled:** clean targets gave a big early gain (held pot and lid 0.52 to 0.76 without learning), but 0.5 mm of target noise removed it. RL learned faster from solved plans but did not end better.
- **Pack swaps are noisy:** expected gains under about 0.02 per episode are coin flips on the board. Confirm a swap on two machines, because a policy can look better on one machine (eval 30: 0.938 on the box, 0.768 fresh).
- **Mesh choice:** choosing a mesh by how its pose tracks public mocap beat choosing by scan match. Both use ground truth and were disclosed.
- **Flips:** a near-symmetric board can fit the scorer's scan turned 180 degrees, costing the pose boards and CD-O. A no-flip gate across scan preparations was added after this.
- **Tracking with new templates is fragile:** the tracker is tuned to its old templates. Swapping them lost objects (-0.170 on 31 Oct, -0.043 on 2 Nov).
- **Rank arithmetic:** each board ranks us separately; the rank sum combines them. One more SP-SR episode is worth about 4 rank points, because five teams stood at 1.00.
- **Leaders:** richchentw's RPE 0.72 cm, MPPE 0.82 cm, no failed episodes and CD-O 0.21 suggest a near-perfect object pipeline; their method is unknown. Results are provisional until the organisers re-run each container.
