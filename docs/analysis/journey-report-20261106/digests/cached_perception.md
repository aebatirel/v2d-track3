# Perception: object detection and identity, 6-DoF tracking, stereo depth, camera/VO and room map, hands, spoon, symmetry and markers, physics-informed perception, and perception QA (4 Oct to 3 Nov 2026)

### overview
The research notes were written on 4–7 Oct (notes 01–21). They found that NVIDIA's ego baseline uses only monocular MoGe-2 depth, tracks one object and assumes 30 fps (note 01). The scorer turns any error in the slot-0 frame-0 pose, any lost object or any shared drift into a large loss: the static predictor scores AUC 0.446 and dropping the most-moving object scores 0.550 (note 02). The literature favoured the 75 mm stereo pair: HOT3D recall@10 cm is 96.8 % with stereo against 30.2 % with monocular depth (note 03). Three trap areas were studied ahead of time: symmetric anchors, spoon frame and roll, and camera drift. Their proposed fixes were a marker correction layer, a room map and a parametric spoon template (notes 12, 13, 15, 17, 18, 20). Building started on 13 Oct (Colab, then Vast from 20 Oct). The stack became FoundationStereo depth (half resolution) + SAM2 proposals scored by DINOv2-L CLS against grey renders of each mesh (the CNOS-style "perc2" detector, which replaced OWLv2 after OWLv2 failed on the grey mono frames) + FoundationPose register/track + keyframe SIFT/stereo PnP VO. Public mean AUC rose from 0.809 (13 Oct Colab, perception only) to 0.932 with the compactness prior (20 Oct). Under the newer registered scoring with our own meshes, the default tracker scored 0.868 on 20 episodes on 25 Oct and 0.945 on 19 episodes on 2 Nov (library_v3 templates + TRACK_GUARD). Gains came from rules added after failures were seen, each validated on public data under a pre-registered no-regression rule: detection rules (78 → 97 of 116 labelled public instances), the gated re-proposal (fixed eval 30's sweeper on the wrist watch, board AUC 0.57468 → 0.58252), the on-table rule (ep11 0.102 → 0.898), TRACK_GUARD (8-episode mean 0.7239 → 0.7821; ep0 lid 0.496 → 0.964), the GT-free board knob identity rule (eval 25; ep23 0.041 → 0.663) and the guard_bridge repair of eval 14's 19 cm cup jump.

From 30 Oct to 3 Nov, about 20 GT-free perception improvements were tested on the 20 public episodes, each with a pre-registered gate. All but the mesh choice and placement steps failed or were not adopted. Failed: the marker correction layer (−0.083), the rest-projection pose fix (−0.0107), re-tracking with per-episode templates (dR −0.170), an RTS smoother (−0.00011), physics priors (best +0.00298 against a +0.003 bar), mask-guided tracking (−0.0009), full-resolution depth (−0.0079), a SAM-6D-style detector (+0.0010 against +0.005), a complete-base pitcher template (−0.249), a rest-segment depth fit (−0.0088), KLT point tracks (−0.058), room-map cameras (G1 bound 0.0029 against 0.01; G2 failed on ep22 −0.0164), tracking with the submitted meshes (−0.043), placement for all objects (+0.0026), H1 bias calibration (only the pitcher passed), an instance pot network (−0.065 / −0.0085) and hand-aware ray correction (holdout −0.0042). Oracles bound what remained: rest-frame poses +0.032, a constant per-object offset +0.030 (track level) or +0.050 (reference level), frame-0 tilt +0.040 (reference level) or +0.0164 (exact tilt), and held-phase ray error +0.0195.

The measured picture: the tracker is accurate (median 0.3–2.3 cm when detection is right), camera drift is small (2–9 mm on 19/20 eval clips) and public loss is dominated by frame-0 detection and identity failures plus systematic biases (template shape, pitcher tilt, along-ray offsets). Smoothing cannot remove those, and depth fitting cannot separate them from template error. The one perception-side lever that kept paying was making the submitted mesh and its placement agree with our own trajectory. The kit registers our mesh to the hidden scan, so the mesh acts as a pose correction on every frame. That is payload-only and needs no retrain. Pre-registered public pose-CV mesh choices gave pack 13p (+0.0103 board AUC) and the eval-20 pitcher placement from depth gave 13p2 (+0.0015). The final best AUC is 0.88571 (14p). On 3 Nov the coordinator recorded the inference that the FoundationPose + stereo tracker is at the limit of cheap fixes [I].

### learnings_task
- The kit aligns the whole achieved scene once, at frame 0, on the first object without a symmetry axis (rotation) and slot 0 (translation). Any anchor error reaches every object and frame, while RPE is blind to common-mode error (note 02; spoon README).
- Since about 21 Oct the kit registers our submitted mesh to the hidden scan (scale-normalised trimmed ICP) and converts our poses through it. Mesh shape and placement in the tracker's body frame therefore act as a pose correction on every frame. Payload-only mesh changes reach the board without retraining; this was the one perception-side lever that kept paying (13p +0.0103, 13p2 +0.0015).
- Every public catastrophic failure started at frame 0 (wrong proposal, below-table registration, look-alike identity swap). No public track showed drift-then-snap. So detection, identity and plausibility guards paid; tracker-level changes did not.
- After the frame-0 fixes, the public loss is systematic bias, not noise: constant offsets (mostly along the camera ray), frame-0 tilt, the pitcher template tilt and a half-frame delay. Smoothers and Kalman filters only re-weight the tracker's poses (−0.0001), while bias oracles are worth +0.030 to +0.050.
- Depth fitting cannot tell template error from tracker error. Fitting to a wrong shape moves a good pose (Stage 1 −0.0088, placement for all objects +0.0026, pose fix −0.0107). Only the pitcher, whose bias is systematic across episodes (dispersion 0.14), gained.
- Public headroom outside a few episodes is small, and the tracker is chaotic (ep18's sweeper is lost by both controls and recovered by every perturbation). A single-episode gain is weak evidence; gates needed means over 20 episodes, a no-harm clause and a noise term (|ΔAUC| ≈ 0.0012 per episode).
- Camera drift was not a bottleneck for perc2: its keyframe PnP on FoundationStereo depth stays within 2–9 mm on 19/20 eval clips. Note 18's +0.10 estimate assumed a much worse VO (20–83 mm). Only public ep24 had real drift (+0.148 with map cameras).
- Look-alike objects (the two wooden boards) are named almost at random by appearance-based detection. A GT-free knob rule fixes it, but it was wired only into the eval-25 job, which left public ep23 at 0.041 for a week.
- The eval scenes contain a second person, and the wearer has a white smartwatch. Cups and the sweeper latched onto the wrist (evals 30, 14, 32, 33). The on-table, reach and re-proposal rules plus guard_bridge handled the visible cases.
- The spoon (10/20 eval episodes, anchor in 5) has no public scan or GT, so no spoon perception change could pass a public gate. The roll frame bit stayed between P(B) 0.33 and 0.87 across analyses.
- Perception changes reach the board only through rebuilt references and retrains, with about 0.74 transfer at $3–8 per episode. Small public gains (< 0.005) were not worth deploying, and fragile tracker changes risked silently changing eval references.
- 70 % of the remaining public AUC loss is in hand-held frames (board merge at assembly, pitcher bias, along-ray errors that depth cannot see). These are not hand problems that depth can correct.

### learnings_tech
- FoundationPose with metric stereo depth and a decent template is very accurate on public objects (median 0.3–2.3 cm). It beat every 'second sensor' we tried: stereo marker fixes (1.0–2.2 vs 0.26–0.42 cm), KLT point tracks (precision 5/17), MediaPipe hand-rigid transport (−0.12 to −0.79), a GDRN-style instance network (−0.065) and physics facet priors (~2°, the same as the tracker).
- FoundationPose is fragile to its inputs: refinement iterations (t4/t8 lose whole objects, −0.25), template changes (Test B −0.043, re-track −0.170, lathe pitcher −0.249), depth resolution (FS1 −0.0079) and GPU nondeterminism (the eval-25 board naming flipped between runs). Its register scores are not comparable across masks (a rack fragment scored 143 vs the true cup's 73).
- Better CD-O meshes are not better tracking templates. Pose-board meshes must be chosen by pose evidence (public CV with 10 registration seeds and a D1 no-flip veto), not by scan Chamfer.
- OWLv2 is unreliable on grey mono frames (4/10). Grounding DINO tiny is the best open-vocabulary detector at frame 0 (10/10 colour, 7/10 grey), but grounding scores cannot be a hard test (a 'wooden spoon' prompt scores 0.32–0.66 with no spoon present). CNOS-style DINOv2 CLS matching against renders plus 3-D gates works; SAM-6D's extra ISM terms added nothing (+0.0010).
- SAM2 video masks fail on some objects (beige cup, white board) and are cut by hands during holds. Restricting depth to them hurts (−0.0077), and propagating from a wrong frame-0 mask poisons re-registration (ep0 lid).
- FoundationStereo at half resolution is the better setting for our pipeline (full resolution −0.0079), at 0.27 s/frame. The time-series error is half-frame delay plus constant bias; a causal Kalman filter adds lag (0 to +22 % worse), offline RTS gives 1–3 %, and a half-frame advance gives −20 % ADD.
- A public-only pycolmap SfM room map relocalises every eval frame (≥ 442 inliers, 1.11 px) in about 22 min of CPU map build. It is an excellent drift detector (84 % of the GT-free bound realised on ep24) but not a general improvement over perc2's near-field PnP.
- MediaPipe 2-D landmarks sit on the real hands (11 px median), but the 3-D lift from stereo collapses during grips (wrist → middle tip 3.8–5.2 cm) and the perceived hand is small (11.9 vs 18.5 cm). Hand-based pose correction is noisier than the object tracker.
- Rendered-only training of a pose network has a large render-to-real gap (ep27 −0.26). Mocap fine-tuning recovers most of it, which is impossible for the spoon. Depth is essential: PnP-only was −0.13.
- Depth-residual corrections fail on thin or flat objects seen obliquely: pixels inside the silhouette hit surfaces behind them (ep24 board shifted 21 cm, sweeper −0.14).
- Infrastructure: a 32 GB RTX 5090 runs out of memory when the guard re-registers with VOS and detection models loaded (the 96 GB PRO 6000 is fine). 'hf download --include' lists the whole store repo (12–15 min per call); prefetch per file instead. ScanNet-like CoACD and platform effects matter for sim assets, not for perception.
