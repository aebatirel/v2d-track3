### GRASP physics retargeting sampler (session_01D9UJC8CjzgvbaFYA5wbvPg, 1 Nov)

Purpose: test whether the GRASP retargeting search (a published 2026 method) beats the lane's own simulator search (FlashCHORD solver) on the public twin of eval 14 (ep13). Eval 14 is the blue cup pick, hand-over to the other hand, flip and placement in a dish rack; the packed policy fails 4 of 10 official tries. Pass rule (fixed before any ep13 run): GRASP must beat our solver by at least +0.05 on our own perceived reference, scored as the mean over 16 replays with small control noise.

- **Setup.** GRASP was re-implemented from its MIT-licensed optimiser code and added as `solve.py --method grasp`; our solver unchanged. Replay reproduced the planned trajectory exactly. On ep31 a tuning run picked GRASP's narrower exploration widths (any gain may come from width, not method).
- **Test A (references as specified by owner).** GRASP minus ours: 0.000 on perceived and 0.000 on oracle — neither solver changed a frame. Reason given: the references park the left hand for the whole clip; the cup rises ~10 cm before the right hand arrives (frame 40–46), so the robot is asked to lift a cup no hand touches. Doing nothing scores 0.554 (perceived) / 0.573 (oracle). Test A arms ran on CPU from 18:10 to about 19:16 on 1 Nov; both failed.
- **Test B (two-hand rebuild, added by the lane).** Perceived: unsolved 0.344, ours 0.394 (±0.146), GRASP 0.418; GRASP − ours **+0.024** (paired 95% range −0.07 to +0.12; GRASP ahead on 9 of 16 replays; bar +0.05) — **FAIL**. Oracle: unsolved 0.604, ours **0.651** (sd ±0.09; 14 of 16 replays 0.65–0.70), GRASP 0.624; GRASP − ours −0.027 — **FAIL**. Single noise-free replays showed GRASP ahead (0.627 vs 0.527) but this disappears under 0.5 mm control noise.
- **Why it fails (lane's measured findings).** (1) On perceived motion, every run loses the cup by frames 15–17 whatever the solver; the cup spins up to 140° about its symmetry axis while resting (f8–f30), which no camera or robot can reproduce, and both the own and the leaderboard checks count it. (2) On true motion with both hands, the task is partly doable: the cup stays within ~2 cm through hand-over and is lost at placement. (3) The gap is where the reference puts the hand at the pick-up: our perceived cup is within ~1 cm and 3–8° of the true cup, but the hand–cup relation is off by ~3.6 cm. (4) At this CPU budget (32 candidates, 8 batches versus a designed ~2,000 candidates and hundreds of batches), GRASP's cost model over-fits and almost all proposals are rejected once it reaches its target; it ends as a local search. (5) Our solver's perceived-reference run took 74 min with 4 retries.
- **Fixes tried, all failed or not adopted.** Removing the cup's invisible spin before the grasp lock: perceived 0.521 vs 0.377 without (do-nothing 0.574), but the cup is lost by frame 10–12; true motion 0.572 vs 0.606 (slightly worse). A motion-cleaning filter (`pipeline/hold_filter.py`) on 4 public episodes: accuracy +0.002 (guard passes), robot-follow score −0.018 (needed ≥ +0.02) — **FAIL**. First-picker hand assignment (`--single_hand 2`, eval 14's construction): ep13 oracle 0.672, perceived 0.323.
- **Correction made by the lane.** It had said the spin made the leaderboard lose the cup. A check of the challenge's scoring code showed any rotation of the blue and beige cups about their axis is equivalent on the leaderboard; spin only costs the lane's own check score. Fixing it would make the own score more honest and references easier to follow, but would not move the board directly (commit 5e5989b). A `pipeline/twist_canon.py` tool (removes symmetric-object spin, no GT; tests pass) was pushed (41383b4).
- **Perfect-following session results (reported by that session; relayed).** ep13 one-hand 0.378/0.554 (seeds 42/43), two-hand 0.587/0.553; the two-hand rule passed its +0.05 bar on average (+0.104) but the lane judged it fragile. On eval 14, two-hand scores 0.603/0.581 vs packed 0.727 (no swap). Repairing a 19 cm tracking jump at frame 46 of eval 14 gave a one-hand policy 0.880 ± 0.054 against its own reference (more runs pending).
- **Lane recommendation.** No GPU step from GRASP. Candidate follow-ups (untested, lane's ideas): freeze unobservable spin of symmetric objects in references; build hand assignment from what hands do; feasibility check on every reference. The two-hand training test (single vs two-hand) was estimated at $5.5–7.5, needing the owner's go.

Lane cost: CPU only, $0 for the solver runs; usage meter ~$50 (model tokens). Nothing rented.

## Lessons

- A GT-free pre-registered gate protected the pipeline: most public perception and solver tests in this period failed their gates and none of those changes reached the packs. Keep the gates, and record crashed cells as the pre-registration says.
- Board-identity checks must run in every lane, not only in the eval job: a missing identity check caused ep23 AUC 0.041 on public controls (fixed to 0.663).
- Run-to-run noise is 0.001–0.004 AUC per episode and ±0.15 on the RPE-driven failure boards; a single-seed win (domain randomization on eval 33, GRASP's noise-free replay) disappeared on rescoring. Score with several seeds and more episodes before believing a gain.
- Scan-derived constants leaked into meshes (7 mm marker radius; a 2.5 mm wall value). Audit every parameter's source before a submission (its provenance question is still open in the transcripts).
- A registration gap is cheap to fix but easy to miss: the leaderboard showed our scores while the organisers had no registration and no collaborator access, which the rules make a condition.
- Claims about scoring rules were corrected after a check of the source code (spin is free on the leaderboard). Read the scorer before building a fix around it.
