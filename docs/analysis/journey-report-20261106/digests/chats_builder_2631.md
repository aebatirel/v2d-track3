## Lessons and turning points

- **Measured repeat variance changed the decisions.** The same policy scores from 0.26 to 0.92 across runs; eval 1, 14 and 30 looked good on one submitted run and fell to 0.82, 0.62 and 0.80 on fresh draws. The team began judging every swap on 10-run means, not single runs.
- **Pre-set rules held, and they killed good-looking ideas.** The freeze fix (no-harm failed by 0.0023), the widened blue cup (both checks failed), the depth audit (AUROC 0.600 against 0.65) and the blue-cup mesh (choice rule written after the scan) were all dropped by written rules, not by impression.
- **Official-evaluator checks reversed training-machine numbers.** Eval 1 looked clean on training machines (zero bad runs in 10) but had 5 bad runs in 22 on the official evaluator; the retrain then cleared the bar on the official evaluator (0.924 against 0.822).
- **The biggest single gain came from physics, not perception.** Realistic object masses (the kit's default is 0.3 kg) raised public scores by a mean of +0.090 on the organisers' meshes; the transfer check to our meshes was the gate for retraining.
- **Budget control moved from prose to a ledger.** Three sessions drew on one budget; a shared ledger with planned and actual lines, a $60 hard cap for one night, and fixed session shares followed. A ledger helper bug erased entries once and was repaired.
- **Usage limits set the rhythm.** Five-hour limits paused the builder repeatedly (about 00:00 to 04:45, 15:00 to 17:00, and other windows); stalls were handled by saving state after each step.
- **Leaderboard facts corrected earlier claims.** The leaderboard scores submitted trajectories, not re-runs; an earlier estimate of +0.003 for the eval 33 swap was corrected to +0.0003.
- **Score drift to watch.** Pack 7 scored 0.81460 (7th place); the gap to 5th was about 0.046 at that point, so swaps worth +0.0005 each cannot close it.
