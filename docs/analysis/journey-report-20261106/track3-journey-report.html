The 10 most important lessons

  1. Read the scorer's code first. The kit registers the submitted mesh to the hidden scan and aligns the whole scene once at frame 0. We learned this fully only on 2 Nov; after that, mesh-only changes gave +0.04 AUC with no retraining.
  2. Read the ranking rule first. The final rank is the sum of six board ranks, not AUC alone. We found this on 31 Oct, with CD-O at 18th of 20.
  3. Gross failures first. Objects tracked onto the wrist, swapped look-alike boards, a falling pitcher and failed learners cost far more than cm-level precision. One pack that fixed them gave two thirds of all our points.
  4. The remaining error was bias, not noise. Constant offsets, frame-0 tilt, the pitcher's missing base and a half-frame delay. Smoothing, filtering and "second sensors" cannot remove bias, and every one we tried failed.
  5. Our GT-free proxy (sim_vs_ref) ranks policies well but cannot see a wrong reference. By 3 Nov all 20 rollouts passed the success thresholds against our own references, so the board's remaining failures were reference-vs-truth errors.
  6. Noise decides small swaps. Single draws spread by sd 0.07-0.26; packed draws were lucky (z about 3.2); box draws overstated fresh draws by 0.10-0.17. Pre-registration, the +0.02 swap bar and two-host confirmation protected us.
  7. Look for silent physics failures. The eval-10 floor was a contact-buffer overflow in training (about 6-8 million warnings), not a policy problem. One setting fixed it: +0.020 AUC.
  8. Policies are tied to the shapes they were trained on. A better collision mesh broke grasps that the policy had built on the old shape.
  9. Single-twin or single-seed wins do not transfer. Use leave-one-episode-out public CV with several seeds, and exclude generation-source episodes.
  10. The field moves late and fast. Our AUC rank fell from 4th to 8th in six days without our score getting worse. Small insurance swaps (+0.001) could not keep pace.

3. The task and the scorer: what we learned

3.1 The task in one paragraph

Each episode is a short head-mounted stereo video of a person handling one or two kitchen objects (pots, cups, a lid, a pitcher, a dish rack, a sweeper, a dust pan, two wooden boards, a wooden spoon). We must (1) reconstruct each object's mesh, (2) estimate each object's 6-DoF trajectory from the video, and (3) train a simulated robot hand pair to make the simulated objects follow that trajectory. Kaggle scores the robot's simulated object motion against hidden motion-capture (mocap) ground truth, and scores our meshes against hidden scans. There are 20 public episodes with mocap and scans (for development) and 20 eval episodes without them (for the board). The public set has 5,246 frames (mean 262 per episode) at 20.25 fps, 10 tasks and 10 object types; 13 of the 20 public episodes have two objects. Public mocap holds object poses only: no hands, no camera.

The final rank is the sum of the six board ranks (FAQ). An early research note (6 Oct) assumed AUC alone ranked teams; the first rank-sum check came only on 31 Oct (section 4). Each board keeps a team's best (or selected) submission, so a variant that is worse on five boards and better on one costs nothing. Rank arithmetic (coordinator, 2 Nov): each board ranks us separately and the sum decides, so one more SP-SR episode was worth about 4 rank points while five teams stood at 1.00. Two scores must not be confused: our "own score" (sim_vs_ref, mean of 10 official runs against our own reference, about 0.94 on average at the end) measures how well the robot follows our reference, while the board scores one stored run against real motion, so a high own score does not guarantee a high board score. The scorer also aligns the whole scene once at frame 0, using the first object without a symmetry axis, and fits our submitted mesh onto its scan of the real object, so the submitted mesh changes the pose scores and not only CD-O.

Two "lotteries" inside the scorer (robustness review, 26-27 Oct):

  1. Frame-0 alignment. The kit aligns the whole achieved scene once, at frame 0: rotation from the first object without a symmetry axis, translation from slot 0. Any anchor error reaches every object and every frame, while RPE is blind to such common-mode error. Our frame 0 is the noisiest frame (FoundationPose register: 1.07 degrees median against about 0.5 later), and in rollouts frame 0 is recorded after the first control step. A slot-0 mesh offset (the blue cup, about 1 cm along its axis) moves every object.
  2. Mesh registration. Since about 21 Oct the kit registers our submitted mesh to the hidden scan (_register_object_body: centroid and RMS normalisation, trimmed ICP from 25 starts, seed 1,000,003 x episode + 101 x slot) and converts our poses through it. So mesh shape is a first-order term of every pose board. Measured on 6 public episodes: clean scan 0.987, x1.10 scale 0.987 (scale is free), convex hull 0.947, bottom 20 % removed 0.904, 3 mm noise 0.825, 5 mm inflation 0.608 (wooden piece and sweeper flipped 180 degrees). Even the stable library pitcher spans 0.667-0.833 AUC across 30 registration seeds.

3.3 What the scorer rewards (measured)

  • Continuity beats precision (note 02, public mocap perturbed and scored with the kit, 8 seeds per case): static predictor AUC 0.446 / SP 0.45 / MP 0.00 / RPE 10.87 cm / MPPE 9.31 cm; losing the most-moving object 0.550; 5 cm drift 0.799; slot-0 start 3 cm off 0.740; 10 degree cup spin on a cup anchor 0.714 (30 degrees: 0.279); lag about 1 % AUC per frame; scale errors under 5 % matter little.
  • Pose, not shape, separates teams. Across teams on 25 Oct, AUC correlated with MPPE at Spearman -0.97 (n 18), RPE -0.93, MP-SR +0.93, SP-SR +0.90, and CD-O only -0.51 (n 16). The CD-O leader then was 17th on AUC. But our own mesh was a pose lever through registration (section 8).
  • Failures dominate the unbounded boards. On public data, successful draws have RPE 1.4-3.5 cm and failed draws 10-77 cm. Over 70 public draws the reference itself is only about 10 % of RPE and 16 % of MPPE [D].
  • Board leverage on 2 Nov [M from the board]: one more SP-SR success (0.90 -> 0.95) would have moved SP-SR from 10th to 6th; MP-SR 0.85 -> 6th; RPE -0.05 cm -> 8th; CD-O -0.0144 cm -> 5th.
  • Timing is a silent killer. NVIDIA's default motion_speed 0.5 makes the packer keep source frame about 0.508 x f in row f. With perfect perception and robot, that alone caps public AUC at 0.415 (SP 0.30, MP 0.00), below the do-nothing floor of 0.446 [C, 30 Oct]. We trained at real speed.
  • What Kaggle does not do: nothing is re-run (kit AUC.py); submitted draws are banked. Results stay provisional until the organisers' container reproduces each counted file (PR #215: run.sh reproduce, policy weights for every sequence, matching hashes, a score "within your own run-to-run variation"). The sponsor also runs a held-out evaluation after close on undistributed data, so per-episode hand fixes would not carry over.

3.4 What sim_vs_ref can and cannot see

sim_vs_ref scores a rollout against our own reference with the kit's plain ADD. It is GT-free, so it is the only number available on eval episodes.

  • It works for ranking policies on the same reference. On 27 stored public policies it correlated 0.997 with the registered AUC against mocap (mean difference 0.021); pairs 0.02 or more apart were ranked correctly 63 of 63 times. GT-free RPE against our reference tracked RPE against mocap at r = 0.9998 over 70 public draws.
  • It cannot see a wrong reference. Pack 1 followed our references at 0.710 and scored 0.575. A swapped board identity on eval 25 scores near zero whatever the robot does. The 3 Nov SR audit showed all 20 14p rollouts pass SP-SR and MP-SR against our references while the board shows 2 SP-SR and 4 MP-SR failures.
  • It cannot rank small differences. Pack 9b's +0.0018 proxy gain became -0.0008 on the board. A proxy difference of about 0.02 on one episode carries no sign information about the board [I].
  • It charges cup twist that the board ignores (symmetric minus plain sim_vs_ref at most +0.009 everywhere, so spin is not where losses are).
  • The loss model is not linear. "True AUC about 0.979 x sim_vs_ref" holds only where the reference itself scores about 0.93 or more; at high sim_vs_ref, true is about 1.025 x S x R. Only about 74-76 % of the pack 3 -> 4 gain reached the board.

3.6 Facts about the data

  • Objects and weights (eval roster): wooden spoon 32.5 %, white pot 15 %, blue cup 12.5 %, dish rack 12.5 %, dust pan 7.5 %, beige cup 5 %, sweeper 5 %, lid, pitcher and the two wooden pieces 2.5 % each.
  • The spoon is in 10 of 20 eval episodes and anchors the scene in five (evals 32, 33, 35, 36, 37). It has no public scan and no public mocap. A 10 degree roll error on an anchor costs 0.09-0.28 AUC on those episodes (eval 32's ratio drops to 0.72; 20 degrees: 0.34). Its frame sign stayed unresolved: P(frame B) estimates ranged from 0.09 to 0.87 across notes. Observed length 30.0-30.7 cm vs template 30.7-31.0 cm; 0 registration flips in 300 fits.
  • Symmetric and look-alike objects. Cups are featureless: spin is visible only through mocap markers (beige cup with two markers flips in 21-28 of 40 frames). The two wooden boards are look-alikes that appearance-based detection names almost at random; a swap alone drops a 0.795 track to 0.057. The rack's marker spacing (153.5 mm) matches the spoon's (154.3 mm). Flat near-symmetric objects flip during registration.
  • Camera timing. Cameras b and c are hardware-synchronous (-0.01 +/- 0.09 ms, 39 clips); camera a pairs a[k+1] with b[k] in 36 of 39 clips; rolling shutter is 31.6 ms per image height. capture_time steps average 50.0 ms with sd 1.0-4.3 ms per episode. Mocap row k sits about 25 ms after camera frame k, and our perceived paths are 25-28 ms (about half a frame) late.

6.5 Grasp locks, carry and hand-overs

  • grasp_lock.py keeps one carrier hand. On public ep13 the reference parked the left hand at z = 57.0 cm for all 190 frames while the cup rose 9-12 cm. Eval 14's packed reference was also single-hand, while the human hands over at about f40-46: exactly the measured failure onset (f42-45 in 7 of 10 draws). So "control-limited" may have been "construction-limited" [I].
  • The lane finding that the grasp-lock "fine-tune" is a fresh 70M run on a reference that already carries the object, which ends before the training assist is fully off, so those policies never train under the exact scoring conditions [H].
  • Hand-held repairs that passed: eval 16's right hand kept on the spoon through release (+0.124) and the guard bridge on evals 14 and 32 (section 7). Two-hand references and a first-picker lock did not transfer to eval.
  • Lift-window resets (training starts sampled around the grasp and lift windows) passed on eval 20 and, by owner waiver, eval 15; 1 of 8 eval tries passed on its own merits.
  • Carry-aware ideas (note 26): the kp 25 N/m wrist has a natural frequency of about 0.64 Hz with an object and saturates on 23 of 27 moving public tracks; kp of 100 or more removed saturation in reruns; an 18 mm contact threshold can mislabel a carry as "no contact"; a 16-30 run wrist-gain study ($35-100) was planned and never approved. Restricting the wrist residual during holds (DexMachina-style) was never tested. From the builder chat (27 Oct) [E, CPU model]: median hand error while carrying and hand jump after release were 11.9 cm and 1.4 cm for the default PD, 3.7 cm and 0.5 cm for kp 100, 8-9 cm and 11-14 cm for a PID on the soft wrist, and 0.6 cm and 0.2 cm for feed-forward. PID was rejected (unstable at strong gain, slow grip build-up, pushes after release). A dropped object costs about 0.3-0.6 AUC on its episode, roughly 0.015-0.03 on the 20-episode mean; a lag of 0.2 s costs about 0.07 AUC and 0.3 s about 0.11. Rule: keep kp 100 only if it wins on at least 3 of 4 episodes. The soft wrist is 12-14 times softer than DexMachina's (300-350 N/m) and 40 times softer than SPIDER's.
  • Hands in replay: moving hands knock resting objects (eval 5 pot 25.7 vs 3.3 mm parked; eval 8 pot 41.7 vs 8.9 mm; eval 33 spoon 87.0 vs 10.6 mm), but these episodes already scored 0.94-0.97, so no rebuild.

7. Robot movement: policies, training and following

The robot is a simulated dexterous hand pair (the kit's Sharpa embodiment) in MuJoCo-Warp. For each eval episode we train a policy with reinforcement learning (RL) to make the simulated objects follow our reference (section 6). Kaggle scores the submitted rollouts as they are; nothing is re-run.

Two measures appear everywhere in this section:

  • sim_vs_ref: how well a rollout follows our own reference (plain ADD-AUC, no ground truth). It is GT-free, so it can be used on eval episodes. On 27 stored public policies it correlated 0.997 with the registered AUC against mocap (mean difference 0.021), and pairs that differed by 0.02 or more were ranked correctly 63 of 63 times [D]. It cannot see a wrong reference, and it charges cup twist that the board ignores.
  • Draws: one official evaluation of a checkpoint is one draw. Draws of the same checkpoint differ a lot (section 10).

8. Meshes, collision geometry and CD-O

A mesh does three jobs in this project, and the best mesh for one job is often not the best for another:

  1. Tracking template: FoundationPose needs a mesh to track an object (section 5).
  2. Submitted mesh: the kit scores it for CD-O and, since about 21 Oct, also registers it to the hidden scan before scoring the poses (section 3). So the submitted mesh moves all six boards.
  3. Simulator collision copy: the physics engine needs convex pieces to simulate contacts. Only the simulator sees this copy.

CD-O numbers below are in cm, computed by the kit's Chamfer distance after a Sim(3) fit (scale, rotation, translation) against the public scan of the same object type, unless marked otherwise. Public-scan scores are [D] (they use ground truth for scoring, never for inference).

9.5 Patterns

Most failures were small misses under pre-registered gates, not disasters: physics fusion missed by 0.00002, the eval 33 bridge by 0.0004, the eval 20 retrain by 0.0004, GRASP by 0.026, the room map by 0.007. Run-to-run noise (about 0.0012-0.0015 per public episode, 0.07 per training cell) set the gates, and the gates held. Four themes repeat:

  1. Bias, not noise. Every smoothing, filtering, re-weighting or "second sensor" idea failed because the remaining error was a constant offset, a tilt or a template error that those methods cannot see.
  2. The tracker is fragile to its inputs. Template, depth, mask and iteration changes all broke whole tracks somewhere.
  3. Policies are tied to the shapes they were trained on. New collision meshes broke 6 of 7 retrains, and box draws overstated fresh draws.
  4. Single-twin or single-seed wins do not transfer. The lathe pitcher, the two-hand public gain and the lift-window public gain all rested on one twin or one collapsed control seed.

10.3 More noise evidence from the chat sessions

  • Same reference, different machine. A no-action replay of ep12 scored 0.656 on one box and 0.560 on another with the same reference (23 Oct): GPU randomness alone is about 0.10 [M]. Eval 1 and eval 30 policies had zero bad runs in 10 on training machines but 5 bad runs in 22 on the official evaluator (30 Oct), which is why confirmation moved to the official evaluator.
  • Warm-start contamination (24 Oct, commit bbe57e6). The simulator's warm-start state survived env.reset, so the same edit scored 0.41-0.59 depending on what had run before it, against 0.508 with sd 0.0 in a fresh batch. The 0.584 seen in one in-process search was not reproducible (a replay gave 0.352 and 0.354). Every CPU search number from before the fix is unverified. A related robustness test on ep18 (K jittered copies per candidate): jitter 1e-3 gave mean 0.8412 (sd 0.0509), jitter 1e-2 gave mean 0.4627 (sd 0.3369), so the ep18 edit is chaotic at the 1 cm scale.
  • Run-to-run spread on one training. Ep12 scored 0.890, 0.860 and 0.858 in three trainings and 0.604 once; seed spread was up to 0.14 on hard episodes and about +/-0.04 on easy ones (22 Oct). The seed 42 / seed 43 single-rollout curves on ep12 (FDR) differed by 0.02-0.16 at the same step count.
  • Small swaps are coin flips. Expected gains under about 0.02 per episode could not be separated from noise on the board (pack 9b: +0.0001 expected, -0.0008 measured). A policy can look better on one machine and not on another (eval 30: 0.938 on the box, 0.768 fresh), so every swap needs two machines. Inside a pack the typical run counts, not the lucky one: pack 6's packed run was 0.916 against a 10-run mean of 0.876, and choosing a draw by its board score is tuning on the test set.

10.4 Variance sources the lane sessions added

  • A reference build is a noise source of its own [M]. Two seeds trained on one reference agreed to 0.001, but references built on different boxes differed by up to 0.31 (embodiment lane, 31 Oct). A result from one reference says little about the recipe; build a reference per seed or read results per reference.
  • A single replay of a reference misleads. ep2's grasp-lock reference scored 0.141 on one replay and 0.260 +/- 0.071 over noisy copies; ep18's solved and normal references were identical yet their runs differed by 0.07.
  • Lift-window retrains show the same pattern on the boxes (seed 42 against seed 43: ep20 0.297 / 0.921, ep28 0.864 / 0.651; full list in section 7.10).
  • Clock labels slipped. Several agents' timestamps were 5-35 min off and were corrected later (the coordinator's ran an hour ahead on 31 Oct); the owner then asked for times in Turkish time (UTC+3). Where a lane's clock label conflicts with the Kaggle record, the Kaggle record is used.

Three general lessons about the 2026 toolset:

  1. Foundation models made a strong stack cheap to assemble, but not easy to improve. The October novelty review rated the method about 1.5-2 of 5 as research (integration of off-the-shelf parts). The defensible new pieces were GT-free self-checks (sim_vs_ref, the plausibility flag, the knob identity rule) and the registration-aware mesh choice.
  2. The weak links were glue, not models: timing conventions, slot orders, contact budgets, frame conventions, packing and identity. Several failed silently without any kit error.
  3. Reported paper numbers did not predict our gains. The marker layer (+0.03 to +0.12 on an emulated prior) gave -0.083 on the real tracker; SAM-6D-style detection and a GDRN-style network added nothing over FoundationPose with good meshes.
