## 11. What we learned about current technology

Each entry: what it did well, where it broke, and the evidence. Licence notes come from the October survey (note 03): FoundationPose, FoundationStereo, BundleSDF, UniDepth, MASt3R-SLAM, WiLoR, HaWoR, GoTrack and SPIDER are non-commercial; MANO is research-only; SAM2.1, Grounding DINO, MoGe-2, DROID-SLAM, MegaPose and GigaPose are OSI-clean. CoTracker3 was rejected for its CC-BY-NC licence.

Three general lessons about the 2026 toolset:

1. **Foundation models made a strong stack cheap to assemble, but not easy to improve.** The October novelty review rated the method about 1.5-2 of 5 as research (integration of off-the-shelf parts). The defensible new pieces were GT-free self-checks (sim_vs_ref, the plausibility flag, the knob identity rule) and the registration-aware mesh choice.
2. **The weak links were glue, not models:** timing conventions, slot orders, contact budgets, frame conventions, packing and identity. Several failed silently without any kit error.
3. **Reported paper numbers did not predict our gains.** The marker layer (+0.03 to +0.12 on an emulated prior) gave -0.083 on the real tracker; SAM-6D-style detection and a GDRN-style network added nothing over FoundationPose with good meshes.
